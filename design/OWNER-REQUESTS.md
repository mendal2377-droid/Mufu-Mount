## Open work, in order (updated 22 November 2026)

1. **Item 40, real buildings and park trees** (CMAB, canopy map): built (build 11) and every test passes; committed locally. The screenshots show a gap: CMAB finds only about half the buildings OSM maps (66 % of their area), so blocks near Mochou Lake that the made-up slabs filled are now partly empty. **The owner chose real outlines only (9 Nov):** the outlines are now grown by the measured 0.3–1.4 m (median size now 1.00 of OSM's), and the gaps stay. In the city since build 12 (11 Nov), with the gate (design/CMAB-CANOPY-PLAN.md, "Results").
2. **Item 43, Xuanwu Gate as it really looks:** in the city and checked (re-exported 14 Nov): three round arches in blue-green rings with smooth vaults, the plaque with its three gold characters (written right to left, an assumption), red middle doors with gilt studs, balustrade, green-roofed tower; the wall for 150 m each side level with the gate; masonry courses on the gate stone and the Ming wall; the approach paved, not asphalt. The walk through a side arch passes (704 m). **Build 14 (22 Nov), the wall's city face:** a stone footing 1.2 m high along the Ming wall's foot (a walker's note on the Xuanwu Gate to Jiefang Gate stretch says the base is stone blocks; the height is our guess); the bricks bluish grey with the odd grey-brown, white, red or yellow one (the same note; the shares are our guess); 929 trees along both feet of the wall, only where the canopy map shows trees and as tall as it says, so the stretch by the gate stays mostly open. Before-and-after views: test-results/shots/before-wall-*, after2-wall-*. **Still open:** no ivy and no buttresses on the city face; where the wall steps up from the gate's level it is one sheer vertical step, which we have no photograph of; the plaque's reading direction is unconfirmed (a search on 22 Nov found Cai Yuanpei's 1929 inscription, thenanjinger.com, but no photograph or text that settles its direction).
2a. **Landmark pass, one by one (owner, 14 Nov):** done and checked in the browser (15 Nov): Jiming Temple and Yuejiang Tower now stand in the city (they were ordinary blocks), the Drum Tower at Gulou is new, the Presidential Palace gate has its inscription, capitals and iron gates, Zhonghua Gate is walked through its four vaulted passages, the Bao'en tower is a glass pagoda. Second batch (15 Nov, checked in the browser): Yuejiang Tower's podium stair now reaches the hillside and the avatar walks up it to the podium top; Mochou Lake's garden (the Shengqi pavilion, Yujin Hall's walled court with its pond, rockery and white statue, Huayan Hall, the ferry pavilion) replaces the city blocks there; the Sacred Way's animals and Wengzhong Road's figures are re-sculpted (humps, trunks and tusks, manes, horns, saddles, court tablets, swords). Third batch (16 Nov, checked in the browser): Toutuo Ridge, Purple Mountain's second top where the cable car arrives, has its three-tier viewing platform with railings, a grey-white stone gateway on the path from the station (walked under), the seated bronze Maitreya and bare boulders; the avatar walks from the cable car station up to the platform. Sizes, the gateway's place and the statue's shape are read from photographs and travel notes, not measured. The trees on Yuejiang's stair are gone. At the station the road stood up to 1.3 m proud of the grass on its downhill side, so the way up went round it; since 19 Nov it lies on the slope, 0.3 m over the grass, and the way up from the station is straight (walked to the platform top). Fourth batch (17 Nov, checked in the browser): Laomendong's archway (老門東, "Laomendong") is now the pale granite paifang of the photographs, with four columns rising into carved round finials, three dark-tiled roofs, the name board, dark couplet boards, drum stones, and a terrace with steps on both faces (walked through a side bay). It was a 7 m frame. Its 17 m height comes from travel guides; the spacings are scaled from a photograph, not measured. Four in five houses in the district are now blue-grey brick with dark brown shopfronts, as in Nanjing's own old town, not white. The lone wall left on Toutuo Ridge's lawn by the third batch is gone. Fifth batch (18 Nov, checked in the browser): the thin black line along the horizon is gone. It was the Mufu sky's painted far shore, which the city and the landmark worlds shared over their own modelled land; the Mufu scene keeps it. The South Bridgehead Park (南堡公园, "Nanbao Park") by the Yangtze Bridge has OSM's footways, 1.1 km of paved paths and loops across its lawns, with trees kept off them. The avatar walks from the riverside spawn to the bridgehead plaza. The paths now lie 0.3 m over the lawn, not 0.15 m: seen from the air they broke into dashes on one slope (fixed 19 Nov, checked in an aerial shot). The park's look beyond those paths (its lawns, the glass walkway a travel guide mentions) is not modelled from evidence. Sixth batch (18 Nov, checked in the browser): the Presidential Palace's surroundings (总统府, "Zongtongfu"). The city leaves a 310 by 320 m box round the palace free of its own blocks, roads and trees, but the palace model fills only the central axis and the West Garden, so the side courts stood bare grey. They now hold 19 single-storey grey-brick halls with dark tiled roofs, one on each OSM footprint there and, where OSM maps none, on CMAB's satellite-traced rooftops (footprints over 800 m2, a hotel among them, are modern blocks and get no hall), and the palace world's own trees. Those trees were wrong until 20 Nov: all 293 stood stacked on one point at the palace's centre (the city read their positions before they were in a scene); since build 13 they stand where the palace model plants them, 279 in the box after the city's cull under roofs, checked from the air. The halls' look is assumed from the complex's old side courts, not surveyed, and each stands on its footprint's rectangle. **The landmark pass is complete.**

Checked in the browser (tests/seats-browser.mjs): every bench placed, reachable on foot, sat on, facing its view; shots of each seated view. Not placed: Mufu Mountain's two wooded viewpoints and the wall top. **Step 4 begun (23 Nov): street life**, researched and planned in design/STREET-LIFE-PLAN.md. Slice 1 done and checked: cars and e-bikes on the city's roads, as many as Nanjing's published peak counts give for each road class and the hour (622 cars and 1,033 e-bikes round Xinjiekou at 8:30 at the counted flows; since the owner chose a calmer street, 290 and 357; tests/traffic-browser.mjs). Remodelled after the owner's views (23 Nov): Kenney's CC0 car models stretched to real sizes, standing on the road surface; the real mix (SUVs, saloons, MPVs, taxis, vans, green plates, Meituan and Ele.me riders); traffic lights at 328 junctions with queues behind them, and turning into the next street (design/STREET-LIFE-PLAN.md). None yet on the elevated roads. Since 23 Nov the traffic lights are drawn (662 signal heads, lit with the simulation). **Slice 2 done and checked (23 Nov): people and boats.** Walkers on the footways, pedestrian streets and Xuanwu Lake's paths (the lake's from its 52,000 visits a day; the streets' ours), and about 60 boats out on Xuanwu Lake from 9:00 to 17:00 (pleasure boats, kayaks, paddleboards), all at the calmer setting (tests/people-browser.mjs). **Slice 3 done and checked (23 Nov): birds.** Land birds in the trees and on the ground round the walker, about 22 a hectare of green as a Beijing park survey found, as Nanjing's commonest kinds (sparrows, bulbuls, blackbirds, spotted doves, magpies); they fly up into the trees when the walker comes close and roost unseen at night. Xuanwu Lake's winter flocks where they were reported: 150 mandarin ducks and 100 night herons at Heping Gate, 150 coots (300 at dusk) and 35 cormorants off the north shore by the station, 40 mallards; none of the migrants in summer (tests/birds-browser.mjs). **Slice 4 done and checked (23 Nov): cyclists.** Not in Xuanwu Lake's park: its regulations have banned all vehicles, bicycles included, since 2010. Instead, riders on the mapped greenways: Purple Mountain's greenway and the cycleways near it, sport teams on road bikes and riders on shared bikes, pedalling, about 5 a kilometre at the busiest hour at the calmer setting (from a count on Nanchang's riverside greenway; no Nanjing count exists). The Yangtze dyke road is mapped but lies outside the walkable world, so no riders there yet (tests/cyclists-browser.mjs). Next: birds in flight overhead.
