# 金陵拾遗 — the facts behind the lanterns

Every lantern in the explorable city game carries one fact. This file lists them with the public source each cites, so a reviewer can check or replace them. The source is in `src/city-game.js` (`FACTS`); keep the two in step.

## Honesty notes

- Facts were gathered from public encyclopaedic and official pages through web search in October 2026. They are short paraphrases, not quotations.
- Several (notably Yuejiang Tower) were taken from search-result summaries rather than a page I read in full. **Treat every entry as "cited, not independently verified" until a human has checked the source.**
- Numbers (heights, years, areas) differ between sources. Where in doubt, the text avoids false precision.
- Reference photographs from Wikimedia Commons were looked at to judge landmark shapes only. None is redistributed; licences are recorded in `PHOTO-REFERENCES.json`.

## Mufu mountain (`mufu`)

1. The Muyan riverside runs about 5.7 km along the south bank, its decks facing Bagua Island.
   - Source: https://www.njqxq.gov.cn/lydt/202211/t20221128_3767167.html
2. Yanji Evening Glow, one of the Forty-Eight Scenes of Jinling, belongs to this shore.
   - Source: https://www.njqxq.gov.cn/lydt/202211/t20221128_3767167.html
3. The ridge’s historic peak once reached 205 m before decades of limestone quarrying cut it down.
   - Source: https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E5%B9%95%E5%BA%9C%E5%B1%B1

## Yangtze bridge (`bridge`)

1. Opened in 1968 as the first heavy bridge over the Yangtze designed and built with Chinese expertise.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Yangtze_River_Bridge
2. A double-deck steel truss: four road lanes above, twin railway tracks below. The main bridge runs 1,576 m.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Yangtze_River_Bridge
3. Each 70 m bridgehead tower carries three steel red flags and sculptures of workers, peasants and soldiers.
   - Source: https://www.tour-beijing.com/blog/jiangsu-travel/nanjing-travel/how-to-visit-nanjing-yangtze-river-bridge

## Yuejiang tower (`yuejiang`)

1. Zhu Yuanzhang planned a tower here in 1374 after his victory at Lion Hill. It stayed on paper for over 600 years.
   - Source: https://www.ourchinastory.com/en/11907/Legend-of-the-first-tower-in-Jiangnan%E2%80%94Yuejiang-Tower
2. Built 1999–2001 in Ming style: 52 m tall and seven levels, though only four show from outside.
   - Source: https://www.trip.com/moments/poi-yuejiang-tower-86069/
3. Red pillars, glazed tiles and gilded ridges. Look for the red balcony on every tier.
   - Source: https://www.trip.com/moments/poi-yuejiang-tower-86069/

## Xuanwu lake (`xuanwu`)

1. About 444 hectares with a 15 km shoreline, and five islets joined by bridges.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanwu_Lake
2. The largest imperial lake garden in Chinese history, with over 2,300 years of recorded past.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanwu_Lake
3. Each island has its season: willows on Huan, cherry blossom on Ying, chrysanthemums on Liang.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanwu_Lake

## Jiming temple (`jiming`)

1. First built in AD 527; the temple you see dates from the Ming, in 1387.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiming_Temple
2. The Medicine Buddha Pagoda is 44.8 m tall: seven octagonal levels, double eaves and copper roof tiles.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiming_Temple
3. A cherry-blossom avenue climbs beside it, and the old city wall at Taicheng looks over Zifeng and Xuanwu Lake.
   - Source: https://www.chinaeducationaltours.com/guide/nanjing-jiming-temple.htm

## Zifeng tower (`zifeng`)

1. 450 m tall, designed by Skidmore, Owings & Merrill and completed in 2010.
   - Source: https://www.som.com/projects/zifeng_tower
2. Its stepped, triangular form shows what is inside: offices and shops below, a hotel, restaurant and public observatory above.
   - Source: https://www.som.com/projects/zifeng_tower
3. Look for the thin mast on top: the shaft narrows in set-back steps, each shifted off-centre.
   - Source: https://www.skyscrapercenter.com/building/zifeng-tower/165

## Purple mountain (`zijin`)

1. At 448.9 m it is the highest mountain in Nanjing.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Mountain_(Nanjing)
2. Purple and golden clouds often wrap its peaks at dawn and dusk, which is where the name comes from.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Mountain_(Nanjing)
3. Its observatory, built to Sun Yat-sen’s last wish, was the first designed by Chinese people.
   - Source: https://www.agatetravel.com/mausoleum-of-dr-sun-yat-sen.html

## Zhongshan mausoleum (`zhongshan`)

1. Exactly 392 stone steps climb from the memorial archway to the sacrificial hall.
   - Source: https://www.chinaeducationaltours.com/guide/nanjing-sun-yat-sen-mausoleum.htm
2. Blue glazed roofs over white granite: “blue sky, white sun”.
   - Source: https://www.chinaeducationaltours.com/guide/nanjing-sun-yat-sen-mausoleum.htm
3. Lü Yanzhi won the commission from more than forty entries; building ran from 1926 to 1929.
   - Source: https://www.chinaeducationaltours.com/guide/nanjing-sun-yat-sen-mausoleum.htm

## Ming Xiaoling (`xiaoling`)

1. The tomb of the Hongwu Emperor, founder of the Ming: begun in 1381, finished in 1405, with about 100,000 labourers.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiao_Mausoleum
2. A 1.8 km Sacred Way is lined with twelve pairs of stone guardians, from lions and camels to elephants and qilin.
   - Source: https://www.chinaeducationaltours.com/guide/nanjing-ming-xiaoling-mausoleum.htm
3. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 3 July 2003, and the pattern for Ming and Qing imperial tombs.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiao_Mausoleum

## Presidential palace (`palace`)

1. The site was a Ming prince’s mansion, then the Qing viceroy’s office, then the Taiping “Heavenly King’s Mansion”.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace_(Nanjing)
2. Sun Yat-sen’s government used it as presidential offices in 1912, and the Kuomintang from 1927 to 1949.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace_(Nanjing)
3. The gatehouse has eight Ionic columns on its south face and three arched iron gates; the stone lions came from the viceroy’s old gate.
   - Source: https://www.islamichinatravel.com/destination-guide/nanjing/nanjing-attractions/presidential-palace/

## Qinhuai old town (`qinhuai`)

1. The Confucius Temple was rebuilt in 1034 after a fire and has long been tied to the imperial university.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Fuzimiao
2. Dacheng Hall stands 16.22 m high on a 1.5 m pedestal.
   - Source: https://www.travelchinaguide.com/attraction/jiangsu/nanjing/fuzimiao.htm
3. Burned in 1937 and rebuilt from 1984, it now sits among lantern-lit shops on the Qinhuai River.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Fuzimiao

## Zhonghua gate (`zhonghua`)

1. Three closed courtyards and four arched gates: an enemy that broke the first door could be trapped inside.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhonghua_Gate,_Nanjing
2. Built between 1366 and 1387, it is the largest castle-style city gate in China.
   - Source: https://www.travelchinaguide.com/attraction/jiangsu/nanjing/zhonghua_gate.htm
3. The barbican is an oblique rectangle with a perimeter of about 199 m.
   - Source: https://www.travelchinaguide.com/attraction/jiangsu/nanjing/zhonghua_gate.htm

## Laomendong (`mendong`)

1. Laomendong’s lanes are marked by a stone paifang gateway.
   - Source: https://dfz.nanjing.gov.cn/gzdt/202411/t20241101_4998828.html
2. Stepped “horsehead” gables rise above the whitewashed walls.
   - Source: https://dfz.nanjing.gov.cn/gzdt/202411/t20241101_4998828.html
3. Compare it with Qinhuai next door: no canal here, just stone, lanes and white walls.
   - Source: https://dfz.nanjing.gov.cn/gzdt/202411/t20241101_4998828.html

## Mochou lake (`mochou`)

1. About 47 hectares and over 1,500 years old; it was once part of the Yangtze.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mochou_Lake
2. Named for Mochou, “no worries”, a young woman from Luoyang in a Liang-dynasty poem.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mochou_Lake
3. Legend says Zhu Yuanzhang and Xu Da played chess at Shengqi Pavilion; the stones spelled “long live” and the lake became Xu Da’s.
   - Source: https://www.travelchinaguide.com/attraction/jiangsu/nanjing/mochou.htm

## Nanjing eye (`eye`)

1. 827.5 m long with a 531.5 m main span: the first sightseeing footbridge on the Yangtze.
   - Source: https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Nanjing%20Eye%20Footbridge/928561
2. Two white elliptical towers, tilted 35° to the shore, carry the whole bridge.
   - Source: https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Nanjing%20Eye%20Footbridge/928561
3. It opened on 16 August 2014 and links Jiangxinzhou with Hexi.
   - Source: https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Nanjing%20Eye%20Footbridge/928561

## Third Yangtze bridge (`third`)

1. Officially the Dashengguan road bridge, formerly called the Third Yangtze Bridge.
   - Source: https://jtj.nanjing.gov.cn/bmdt/202402/t20240226_4174172.html
2. Its steel towers curve into an A shape, with fans of cables reaching down to the deck.
   - Source: https://jtj.nanjing.gov.cn/bmdt/202402/t20240226_4174172.html
3. It is not the high-speed railway arch bridge beside it: a different structure with a different job.
   - Source: https://jtj.nanjing.gov.cn/bmdt/202402/t20240226_4174172.html

## Niushou mountain (`niushou`)

1. Named for twin peaks that look like a bull’s horns.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niushoushan
2. The Buddha’s Usnisa Palace is a golden hemispherical dome, said to echo a lotus rising from water.
   - Source: https://www.chinadiscovery.com/jiangsu/nanjing/niushoushan.html
3. Niutou Chan, which began here, was an early step in making Chan Buddhism Chinese.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niushoushan

## Qixia mountain (`qixia`)

1. Founded by the monk Ming Sengshao in the Southern Qi dynasty (479–502).
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qixia_Temple
2. The five-storey octagonal Sheli Pagoda was first built in 601 and rebuilt in 945 under Southern Tang.
   - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qixia_Temple
3. Behind the temple, the Thousand Buddha Cliff carries grottoes from the Qi dynasty to the Ming; maples turn crimson in November.
   - Source: https://www.travelchinaguide.com/attraction/jiangsu/nanjing/qixia_temple.htm
